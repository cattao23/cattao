Overview of Hematology

Hematology is the branch of medicine concerned with the study of the cause, prognosis, treatment, and prevention of diseases related to blood. It involves the study of blood cells (erythrocytes, leukocytes, and platelets) and the coagulation system.

Common Hematological Disorders

Anemia

A condition marked by a deficiency of red blood cells or hemoglobin, leading to reduced oxygen transport.

Leukemia

A group of blood cancers that usually begin in the bone marrow and result in high numbers of abnormal white blood cells.

Hemophilia

A genetic disorder that impairs the body's ability to make blood clots, a process needed to stop bleeding.

Lymphoma

Cancer that begins in the lymphocytes of the immune system, affecting the lymph nodes and spleen.

Diagnostic Procedures

  • Complete Blood Count (CBC): Measures levels of RBCs, WBCs, and platelets.
  • Bone Marrow Biopsy: Extraction of marrow to examine cell production.
  • Peripheral Blood Smear: Visual examination of blood cells under a microscope.
  • Flow Cytometry: Technique used to identify specific types of cells in a sample.

Common Treatment Modalities

Modern hematology utilizes a variety of therapeutic approaches depending on the specific condition:

  • Chemotherapy and Targeted Therapy
  • Stem Cell Transplantation (BMT)
  • Blood Transfusions
  • Anticoagulant Therapy
